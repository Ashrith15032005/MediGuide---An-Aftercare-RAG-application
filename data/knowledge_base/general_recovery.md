# General Recovery After Surgery

## Overview
This guide gives general, non-specific advice for recovering at home after a surgery or procedure that does not have its own MediGuide guide. It cannot cover the details of your particular operation, such as exact lifting limits, movement restrictions, dressings or timelines. Your own discharge summary and your surgeon's instructions always come first. If this guide and your care team disagree, follow your care team.

## Wound Care
Keep the dressing clean and dry and leave it in place until your surgical team says it can come off. Wash your hands before and after touching the area. Once you are cleared to shower, let soapy water run over the wound and pat it dry; do not scrub. Do not soak in a bath, swimming pool or hot tub until the wound is fully closed and your surgeon agrees. Do not apply creams, powders or home remedies unless your surgeon advised it. Do not pick at stitches, skin glue or adhesive strips.

## Activity and Rest
Rest when you are tired, but do not stay in bed all day. Short, gentle walks several times a day help circulation, lower the risk of blood clots and help the bowels recover. Increase the distance a little each day. Do not lift heavy objects, push, pull or strain until your surgeon gives you a limit for your procedure. Avoid strenuous exercise and contact sports until you are cleared at follow-up. Any special movement or weight-bearing restrictions come from your surgeon, not from this guide.

## Driving and Returning to Work
Do not drive while you are taking prescription pain medicine or if you cannot turn, brake or move quickly without pain. The right time to restart driving, work or study depends on your operation, so ask your surgeon. Desk work can often restart sooner than physical work. Ask for written clearance if your job is physical.

## Diet and Hydration
Start with light foods and return to your usual diet as your appetite comes back, unless your doctor gave you a special diet. Eat small meals more often rather than a few large ones. Drink plenty of fluids unless you were told to limit them. Fruit, vegetables and whole grains give fiber that helps prevent constipation. Limit very spicy, oily or fried food if it upsets your stomach. Avoid alcohol while taking prescription pain medicine or antibiotics. Protein-rich foods such as dal, eggs, curd, milk, fish or chicken support healing.

## Bowel Habits
Constipation is common after surgery and after taking pain medicine. Walking, fluids and fiber help. Do not strain. Take a stool softener or laxative only if your doctor or pharmacist recommends it.

## Pain and Medicines
Take medicines exactly as prescribed and at the times written on your prescription. Finish the full antibiotic course if one was prescribed. Taking pain medicine with food can protect the stomach. Keep a written list of all your medicines and bring it to follow-up visits. Questions about changing, skipping or combining medicines should go to your doctor or pharmacist.

## Sleep and Comfort
Rest in the position that is most comfortable and that your surgeon has allowed. Loose, soft clothing that does not press on the wound is more comfortable than tight clothing. Short naps during the day are normal while you recover. Do not smoke, because smoking slows wound healing.

## Follow-up
Keep every follow-up appointment even if you feel well. Bring your discharge summary, your medicine list and any questions you have written down. Ask at the visit when you can restart normal activities, exercise, driving and work, and ask for any test or pathology results.

## Warning Signs
- Fever of 38 degrees Celsius (100.4 degrees Fahrenheit) or higher, or chills
- Redness, swelling, warmth, pus or bleeding from the wound, or a wound that opens
- Severe or worsening pain that medicine does not control
- Repeated vomiting or being unable to keep fluids down
- Pain, swelling or redness in a calf, or sudden shortness of breath or chest pain (seek emergency care)
- Being unable to pass urine, or no bowel movement for more than three days
