# C-Section — Post-Discharge Aftercare Guide

## Diet
Eat a balanced, iron and protein-rich diet to support healing and breastfeeding: dals, leafy greens, eggs, milk, and fruits. Stay well hydrated, especially if breastfeeding. Avoid excessive oily or gas-forming foods (like rajma, chole, or cabbage) in the first 1-2 weeks if they cause discomfort. There is no need for a special "confinement diet" beyond general balanced nutrition unless advised by your doctor.

## Activity and Movement
Gentle walking is encouraged from day 1 to aid circulation and digestion. Avoid lifting anything heavier than your baby for the first 2 weeks, and nothing heavier than 5 kg for 4-6 weeks. Avoid abdominal exercises, driving, and climbing stairs frequently until cleared by your doctor, usually around 4-6 weeks. Avoid sexual intercourse until your doctor confirms healing at the postnatal checkup, typically 6 weeks.

## Sleep
Rest whenever possible, ideally aligning sleep with the baby's schedule. Sleeping on your back or side with a pillow supporting the incision area can reduce discomfort. Avoid sleeping on your stomach for at least 2 weeks.

## Wound and Incision Care
Keep the incision dry and clean; follow your doctor's dressing instructions. Wear loose, breathable clothing to avoid irritation. Showers are generally fine after 24-48 hours, but avoid soaking in a bathtub for 2 weeks. Watch the incision daily for signs of infection.

## Medication
Take prescribed pain relief and antibiotics as directed and complete the full antibiotic course. If breastfeeding, only take medications your doctor has confirmed are safe during lactation.

## Red Flag Symptoms (require immediate doctor contact)
- Fever above 100.4°F (38°C)
- Heavy vaginal bleeding (soaking more than one pad per hour)
- Increasing redness, swelling, or discharge from the incision
- Severe abdominal pain not relieved by prescribed medication
- Foul-smelling vaginal discharge
- Pain, swelling, or redness in the calf (possible blood clot)
- Difficulty breathing or chest pain
