# Appendectomy — Post-Discharge Aftercare Guide

## Diet
For the first 2-3 days, stick to light, easily digestible foods: khichdi, curd rice, boiled vegetables, clear soups, and plain toast. Avoid oily, spicy, and fried foods for at least 1 week, as they can cause bloating and discomfort near the incision. Introduce regular meals gradually starting day 4-5 if there is no nausea or pain. Drink plenty of water and fluids like coconut water or ORS to stay hydrated. Avoid carbonated drinks for the first week since gas can increase abdominal pressure.

## Activity and Movement
Light walking is encouraged from day 1-2 after surgery to prevent blood clots and aid digestion. Avoid lifting anything heavier than 5 kg for at least 2 weeks. No strenuous exercise, running, or gym workouts for 4-6 weeks. Climbing stairs slowly is fine but avoid rushing. Driving should be avoided until you can comfortably wear a seatbelt and make an emergency stop without pain, typically after 1-2 weeks.

## Sleep
Sleep in a comfortable position, preferably on your back with a pillow supporting your knees to reduce strain on the abdomen. Avoid sleeping on your stomach for the first 2 weeks. It is normal to feel more tired than usual during the first week of recovery.

## Wound and Incision Care
Keep the incision site clean and dry. Follow the doctor's instructions on dressing changes. Avoid soaking in a bathtub or swimming pool for at least 2 weeks; showers are usually fine after 48 hours unless advised otherwise. Do not apply any creams or ointments to the incision unless prescribed.

## Medication
Take prescribed painkillers and antibiotics exactly as directed, and complete the full course of antibiotics even if you feel better. Avoid NSAIDs (like ibuprofen) unless specifically approved by your doctor, as they can increase bleeding risk.

## Red Flag Symptoms (require immediate doctor contact)
- Fever above 101°F (38.3°C)
- Increasing redness, swelling, warmth, or pus/discharge from the incision
- Severe or worsening abdominal pain
- Persistent vomiting or inability to keep fluids down
- No bowel movement or passing gas for more than 3 days after surgery
- Excessive bleeding from the incision site
